# Philly Blues Website — Traffic Summary

**Site:** phillybluescfc.com
**Period covered:** Aug 16 – Sep 14, 2026 (from launch through today)
**Source:** Google Analytics 4
**Prepared:** Sep 14, 2026

---

## The Headlines

Since the site went live in mid-August, here is what we have seen:

- **237 people** visited the site (all of them first-time visitors).
- They made **400 visits** in total and viewed the page **442 times**.
- The average visitor spent about **55 seconds** on the page.
- Just under **half of all visits** (47%) counted as "engaged" — meaning the person stayed, scrolled, or did more than glance and leave.

For a brand-new local supporters group, that is a solid start. These are real people finding the site, not just quick bounces.

One caveat worth remembering: our own visits (yours, mine, other officers checking the site) are counted in these numbers too. So the "real" outside audience is a little smaller than the totals above.

---

## Where the Traffic Came From

Here is the breakdown of the 400 visits by how people arrived:

- **Direct — 182 visits (46%).** People who typed the address, tapped a saved link, or came from an app that hid where they clicked from.
- **Social media — 114 visits (28%).** People who came from a social post.
- **Google search — 91 visits (23%).** People who found us by searching.
- **Everything else — small.** A handful from the official Chelsea supporters-clubs site and a few from AI chat tools.

### Which social channel won: Instagram, clearly

Breaking the social visits down by platform:

- **Instagram — 72 visits.** The runaway winner, and the most engaged of any source (about 4 in 5 of those visits were "engaged").
- **X / Twitter — 24 visits.**
- **Facebook — about 16 visits.**

**Instagram drove roughly three times the traffic of X and Facebook combined.** That is the clearest signal in the whole report.

A note on that big "Direct" number: when people tap a link inside the Instagram or Facebook app, it often shows up as "direct" rather than social. So social's real pull is very likely even bigger than the 28% above suggests.

---

## The Busiest Day

**Thursday, August 20** was our biggest day, with 35 visits.

That lines up with the second launch post (the ticketing-changes announcement). The other clear bumps landed around the first match back (Fulham v Chelsea, Aug 24) and the following weekend. In short: **every time we posted, traffic jumped.**

---

## Who Was Visiting

- **Location:** Almost entirely US-based — 95% of visitors. A small number came from the UK, Ireland, Canada, and France.
- **Device:** **84% visited on a phone.** Only about 16% used a computer. (Safari was the most common browser, which fits an iPhone-heavy, mobile crowd.)

The takeaway here is simple: **most people see our site on a small screen.** It needs to look and work great on a phone first.

---

## 3 Practical Takeaways for Next Time

1. **Lean into Instagram.** It is by far our strongest channel and brings the most engaged visitors. Put the most effort into IG posts, stories, and link-in-bio — that is where the audience already is.

2. **Post on a rhythm — traffic follows posts.** Every announcement produced a visible spike. A steady posting cadence (match previews, watch-party reminders, results) will keep visits coming rather than fading between posts.

3. **Keep the site phone-first.** With 84% on mobile, anything important — the pub location, match times, how to join — should be easy to read and tap on a phone without pinching or zooming.

---

## A Few Honest Notes

- Numbers are modest, which is normal for a new local group in its first month. The trend and the *sources* matter more than the raw totals right now.
- Every visitor so far is a first-timer. As the season goes on, watch for **returning visitors** — that will tell us the site is becoming a habit, not just a launch-week click.
- Google's age/gender data is turned off for this property (a privacy setting), so we cannot report visitor demographics.
